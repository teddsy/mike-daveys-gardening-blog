Sample post

Leave wet clay alone

When heavy soil sticks to your boots, stop. On Nottinghamshire clay, digging or walking while it is wet smears the structure, and the bed can stay tight for months. Spread compost or leaf mould on top and let the worms take it in. A no-dig mulch, or a raised bed, sheds the worst of the winter wet and means you are not standing in the mud to sow.

Sample post

A few pots on the windowsill

Late winter is a good time to sow a small tray, not a whole greenhouse. Broad beans, peas, lettuce and onions cope with a cool bright windowsill. Leave tomatoes, courgettes and French beans until the days are longer, or the seedlings grow thin and floppy. If you can read a paper by the window at midday without a lamp, the early sowings have a fair chance.

Sample post

Write the seed list before you browse

Note what you actually ate last year, then what you have room to grow. A packet of kale, a short row of carrots and one good tomato teach more than a drawer of unopened packets. Check the dates on old seed. Parsnip seed is rarely worth keeping. Peas and beans usually are, for a season or two, if they were kept dry and cool.
